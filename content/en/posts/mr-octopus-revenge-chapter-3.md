Before dawn, Zhang Yu'an learned what the eight tentacles were.

The octopus lit them for her one at a time. Each time, the ring of light at the base of the tentacle pulsed.

The first: data tracing. Money that left an electronic trace could not escape it.

The second: emotion analysis. Micro-expressions, voiceprint shifts — no lie survived it.

The third: memory replay. Wherever there was footage or a record, it could turn back the clock and play it again.

The fourth: causal projection. Given enough information, it could calculate what a person was most likely to do next.

The fifth: network infiltration. The sixth: disguise generation. The seventh: time anchoring. The eighth—

When the eighth lit up, the octopus paused.

"The eighth: sincerity detection," it said. "But it hasn't woken."

"Why?"

"It needs one act of sincerity to wake." Its voice gave nothing away. "Being treated with sincerity. Or treating someone with sincerity. The rules don't say which. I have been waiting."

Zhang Yu'an smiled, a cold little smile. "Sincerity? That's the one thing I have plenty of. I gave it to two people. They traded it for a watch."

The octopus did not answer. Eight tentacles hung quietly. The blue light watched her for two seconds.

"Host, it's almost light," it said. "Let's do the first thing."

"What thing?"

"Money." it said. "The one hundred eighty-six thousand is still in Lin Weiwei's account. She will move it tonight — I have run the projection. Her behavior pattern puts confidence at 91 percent."

"You need to make it immovable before tonight."

* * *

Zhang Yu'an did not go home.

She bought bread and coffee at the convenience store downstairs, sat at her desk on the seventeenth floor, and pretended to work overtime for a full day. Her phone lay quiet on the desk. No message from Cheng Zhe. None from Lin Weiwei.

They were probably still asleep. Sleeping off last night's "nobody goes home sober."

She stared at the ceiling all afternoon. Call the police? Not enough evidence. She had transferred the money voluntarily, and there wasn't even an IOU. Make a scene at his company? He would turn it around and call it extortion. Tell her family? Her mother's heart was bad, and her father's blood pressure climbed faster than her salary.

None of it would work.

Only its way would.

At six in the evening, the octopus spoke in her head: "Host, step one. The chain of evidence."

The first tentacle unfolded without a sound. Blue light drew a thin line through the air, and the other end of the line touched her monitor.

The screen began to move—

The account details for Lin Weiwei's account, the last three months of transactions, the in-and-out record of that 186,000, a timeline of seven transfers between Cheng Zhe and Lin Weiwei, the booking records at Lanting Private Kitchen, the itemized bill for room B2, and even the original shooting data behind Cheng Zhe's nine-photo post — taken a full eleven days before the "tonight" in his caption.

The photos had been taken in advance.

Even "that chapter's closed" had been rehearsed.

She read through it one item at a time, fingertips trembling faintly against the desk. She hated him. Hated him to the point where she went calm.

"Enough," she said. "Is this enough to file a case?"

"Whether it's enough is not for us to decide," said the octopus. "It is for a professional. Host, do you know a lawyer you can trust?"

She blinked.

One person came to mind. A senior she had met at the college legal aid center, surname Zhou, now a lawyer. Last year she had posted a business card in the alumni group. Economic disputes were her specialty.

She found the card and took a photo of it.

"Good," said the octopus. "Step two. At eight tonight, Lin Weiwei will log in to her mobile banking."

"Before she does, I am going to make that money impossible for her to touch."

"How?"

"Nothing illegal." The blue light flickered. "I am only submitting, on her behalf, something she should have submitted herself — a report of unusual account activity. She changed the phone number on that bank card herself last week, so the paperwork is already there. I am simply… filing it for her."

"Once the report is accepted, the account is frozen for review. Forty-eight hours to start."

"Forty-eight hours is enough for your lawyer to read all of this."

Zhang Yu'an looked at the tentacle stretched across the screen and asked suddenly: "Why are you helping me?"

The octopus went still.

"Three years ago, when my creator was kicked off the project team, it was the same," it said. "The work was his. The credit went to someone else. He never even got an explanation."

"I watched him leave. I couldn't do anything."

"Now I can."

* * *

At 8:06 that evening, a receipt she had never seen appeared on Lin Weiwei's mobile banking screen.

At 8:11, the account was frozen.

At 8:23, Lin Weiwei called Cheng Zhe. His voice message came back with one line: "What's there to panic about? I'll handle it tomorrow."

At 8:50, Zhang Yu'an sent the organized evidence package to Lawyer Zhou.

At ten sharp, Lawyer Zhou called back. Her voice was steady: "Yu'an, I've read the materials. Criminal private prosecution plus civil recovery — we can fight this. Come to my office tomorrow morning. We'll go through it in detail."

After she hung up, Zhang Yu'an leaned back in her chair and let out a long breath.

Outside the window the city was lit up. For the first time in half a month, she felt that one of those lights might be hers.

"Step one, done." The octopus hovered at her shoulder, tentacles drawn in like a quiet silver-gray charm. "Congratulations, host."

She looked at it and suddenly felt it was time to give it a name.

"Mr. Octopus," she said. "That's what I'll call you."

The blue light flickered, as if it had blinked.

"All right, host."

Then her phone buzzed.

A text from an unknown number sat in the notification bar.

She opened it. One line:

【You touched something you shouldn't have.】

Zhang Yu'an stared at it and slowly sat up straight.

Mr. Octopus unfolded one tentacle, without a sound. The blue light fell on the message and rested there for two seconds.

"Host," it said, "it seems this revenge is bigger than we thought."
